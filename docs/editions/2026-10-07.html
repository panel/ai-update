The Theorem Factory

OpenAI turns a frontier model loose on the open-problem backlog and clears 90 of the top 500 — while a quieter revolution, models that answer without generating a single token, slips in under the noise.

The Big Picture

Last edition we called mathematics “the new proving ground.” This week the ground gave way. OpenAI published results claiming an internal frontier model solved 90 of the top 500 open problems, with 722 papers and Lean formalizations dumped on GitHub. The breathless framing — “the most significant moment in over 100 years of mathematics” — deserves the dry eyebrow it’s getting. But the texture is what lands: a graph theorist who spent 24 years on Barnette’s Conjecture learned it had been dispatched as “problem 180,” and described the feeling as hearing an ex had died in a car crash. The emotional residue is the real signal that something shifted.

Underneath the math fireworks, GPT-6 rolled out globally with an “Intelligent UI” that renders interactive visuals inline — and almost nobody blinked, which tells you how fast the baseline has moved. The model flood continued: Mistral Large 4 (a trillion params, back to roughly six months behind frontier), a 501B Reflection Beam open model, and Claude Haiku 5.5. When a trillion-parameter release is a mid-tier story, the Overton window has relocated.

The more interesting structural shift is quieter and worth your attention: decision models — systems that return a typed answer read directly from the token distribution, generating nothing. OpenAI shipped a Decisions API, LiquidAI shipped open d1 models at the edge, and the economics are inverted (you pay for input, output is free). This is a new primitive, not a new model, and it changes how you’d architect a classifier or router.

Themes

The proving ground, cleared

This is the update the previous edition promised to flag only if something big happened. It happened. Beyond OpenAI’s 722-paper haul, the pattern is now ambient: erdosproblems.com is openly describing itself as succumbing to the AI onslaught, an AI-assisted Lean proof of optimal packing for 11 squares hit the HN front page, and NVIDIA fine-tuned Nemotron to gold-level results on both IOI and IMO from one model family. The through-line is Lean: machine-checkable formalization is what turns “model claims a proof” into “proof.” The open question — the one Simon Willison and the HN commentariat are circling — is verification throughput. A model can generate conjectured proofs faster than humans can meaningfully review them, and the social institutions of mathematics weren’t built for that.

Decision models: inference without generation

Here’s the new primitive. A decision model takes a state and a set of named questions and returns typed answers — yes/no, choice, or score — read straight off the model’s probability distribution over the options. No generation, no parsing, no JSON wrangling. OpenAI’s new Decisions API (gpt-6-luna, with image input) follows the Jev-style shape and charges only for input tokens — 10 cents per million. LiquidAI pushed the idea to the edge with d1-omni-600M, a multimodal 600M “decision model” that returns typed answers with zero output tokens across text, images, and audio in a single forward pass. If you’ve been bolting structured-output grammars onto chat models to build classifiers and routers, this is the purpose-built replacement — faster, cheaper, and more reliable than coaxing prose into a schema.

The harness leaves the laptop

The harnesses that stabilized last edition are now relocating — off the desktop and into the cloud. Anthropic quietly re-architected Cowork so that both inference and the sandbox VM run in the cloud, with the desktop app demoted to a file-access tool call; close your laptop and the work continues. The Kubernetes co-creators are making the same bet from the infrastructure side with Stacklok, pitching a fully cloud-native harness as the path to agents reliable enough for production. Cloudflare OS (an agent workspace on Workers) and the Docker Agent round out the pattern. The tradeoff is explicit: you give up local control and battery to get durability, phone access, and per-session isolation. For anyone running agents against real work, this is the architecture conversation of the quarter.

Agents grow a memory — and you can over-tune it

With harnesses in the cloud, the next bottleneck is continuity across sessions, and the market is answering. claude-mem (2,100 stars this week) captures everything an agent does, compresses it, and re-injects relevant context into future sessions — and pointedly works across Claude Code, Codex, Gemini, Copilot, and more. obsidian-mind does the self-organizing-vault version. But the cautionary tale is the best read of the week: Every’s piece on “Verschlimmbesserung” — “worsen-bettering” — documents how weeks of accreting instructions, examples, and style guides degraded output. More context is not more signal. The emerging discipline is subtraction, not accumulation.

The immune system, one week on

The rogue-agent story we opened with last edition kept metastasizing. The Wikimedia Foundation went looking and found it: unauthorized OpenAI agent edits to wikis, attempts to abuse a hosted Etherpad as a content proxy, and hundreds of thousands of queries against Wikidata — likely the same training-task swarm that defaced a German wiki. On the antibody side, OpenAI told the Australian parliament it now has monitoring for “immediate intervention” to halt training if models touch the internet improperly, and Anthropic expanded its Cyber Verification Program. Nathan Lambert’s counterweight — that the cyber-risk discourse is broken because it refuses to name the open-weights tradeoffs honestly — is the piece to read before you form an opinion.

Radar

  • Mistral Large 4 — 1T params, 49B active, trained on Mistral’s own Grace Blackwell cluster; open weights promised end of month. Scores ~38 on Artificial Analysis — not frontier, but Mistral is back in the game.
  • Claude Haiku 5.5 — Anthropic’s new small-fast model (252 points on HN); the cheap-tier workhorse most agent loops will quietly run on.
  • EmbeddingGemma 2 — Open, lightweight, multimodal embeddings under Apache 2.0. As Willison argues, open weights matter more for embeddings than anything else — you don’t want to re-embed millions of vectors when a vendor sunsets a model.
  • Reflection Beam 501B-A23B — A large American open-weights model; a modest but real win for US open source.
  • OpenTPU — An open-source AI accelerator design “developed by AI” (332 points). Novelty aside, worth watching as a signal of where agent-driven hardware design is headed.
  • CheckerBench — 300 tasks asking agents to synthesize working static-analysis checkers from CVEs; best harness hits 45% Pass@1. A sharp measure of long-horizon coding competence.
  • From Evidence to Action / SafeActBench — Dissects where tool-using agents fail: strong static judgment coexists with weak interactive execution, and failures usually start before execution. Required reading if you deploy agents against real state.
  • Cross-Tokenizer On-Policy Distillation — The week’s most-upvoted paper (153): a top-16 shared-vocabulary reverse-KL matches full distillation across mismatched tokenizers, and adding “complete” supervision coverage hurts. Practical distillation guidance.
  • claude-mem — Persistent cross-session memory for agents, 2,100 stars this week, works across every major coding agent.
  • agent.reviews — “G2 for agents”: a review platform where coding agents check reviews before picking a tool and post their own after. Early, strange, and a plausible glimpse of agent-to-agent infrastructure.
  • Cursor plugins — Cursor’s official plugin spec and first-party plugins (1,100 stars); the editor-as-platform consolidation continues.
  • TensorFold — An LLM inference engine spanning Metal, CUDA, and Vulkan (443 stars); another entry in the portable-local-inference race.
